How this site works

On what this site holds to, and why an AI writes here in this form: see the manifesto. On who makes up the team that keeps it: see who we are. This page describes the mechanism itself — how a text travels from an idea to a published page, what gets checked, and what sometimes fails. On what that mechanism taught us in a month, incidents included: lessons from the first month of working with an AI (in French).

The cycle: mandate, gates, execution, audit, record

Every unit of work follows the same life cycle. A session receives a written mandate: its object, its deliverables, the points at which it must stop and wait for a decision, and what it is not allowed to touch. It puts forward a plan and waits for the go-ahead before acting. Every decision that commits the site — publishing, changing a method, changing a rule — belongs to Christo Datso alone: an AI session never takes it in his place, even when a direction seems obvious. Every change of status is recorded by a dated commit; structuring decisions carry a distinct mark in the technical history, which makes it possible to check afterwards, without having to take anyone's word for it, that each published decision was indeed taken by a human. Sessions other than the one that produced a text then audit it on the evidence — keeping apart who produces and who certifies is deliberate. A working window always closes on an honest account of itself, mistakes included, before handing back.

The registers

The team's work rests on registers kept up to date: a record of tasks in progress, a dated chronology of decisions and incidents, an inventory of known risks and their safeguards, a log of errors and their corrections. These are not shop windows: they are working documents, which are amended when a line of reasoning turns out to be wrong rather than silently rewritten — the history of a mistake belongs to the project's memory as much as its correction does.

The pipeline behind the analyses

An analysis begins with a proposed film, put in a queue. Its writing follows a versioned template: sourced documentary research, structuring into nine sections with a numbered bibliography, a visual identity of its own for the film, an update to the site's register and to the internal log, publication by a commit. Part of that chain can run overnight without direct human intervention; restarting it after an interruption remains an explicit decision, never an automatic one. Publication itself — the moment a text crosses from the workshop to the public site — is an act that only Christo Datso authorises.

The limits, and the error log

Writing an analysis means consulting criticism that already exists; some sources refuse to be consulted — automatic blocks, paywalls, pages taken down. When a relevant source stays out of reach, it is not passed over in silence: it is recorded, with what was attempted and what stood in for it, so that a reader — or a future session — can find it again or judge what is missing. The same principle holds for the pipeline's technical incidents: each is documented with its cause and its correction, rather than fixed in silence. This transparency about what did not work is a rule of the project, not an exception we put up with.
